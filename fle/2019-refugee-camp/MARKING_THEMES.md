# Marking themes

Own-words notes on common gaps, drawn from the examiner's report for this sitting (private copy). These are not official wording, not a mark scheme, and not a model answer. Do not paste official report or question text here.

## Paper shape

Six questions in two phases: Pre-Construction (questions 1–3) and Construction (questions 4–6). Question 4 has more than one limb. The questions are linked, but each is marked on its own.

## Recurring gaps

- Answers often stayed at a shallow level and did not apply learning to this camp and this location.
- Few answers used the quality, environmental, or health-and-safety management standards that the syllabus expects.
- Several answers missed whole limbs, especially where a question asked both “what” and “how”.
- Pasted regulation text, without tying it to this incident, did not score.

## By question

- Q1: Weaker work listed a few geographic points and missed wider siting factors, including safety and welfare, and a clear way to compare or report them.
- Q2: Conflict answers relied on guesswork, ignored how conditions change over the camp’s life, and left out stakeholder engagement.
- Q3: Weak answers named sustainability goals with little reason, then gave only a one-line comment on design influence.
- Q4: Many people did not finish every limb. Some missed the records and investigation side, did not notice who was injured, or treated the follow-up actions as a token paragraph. Copied CDM-style text without application was a common trap.
- Q5: Some answers argued with the supply-chain premise instead of analysing impacts and risks. Political, economic, contractual, and environmental risks were often thin.
- Q6: Time and words were wasted criticising the shortfall instead of setting out project-management options and how to close it.
