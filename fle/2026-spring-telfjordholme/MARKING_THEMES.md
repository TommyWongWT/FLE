# Marking themes

Own-words notes on common gaps, drawn from the examiner's report for this sitting (private copy). These are not official wording, not a mark scheme, and not a model answer. Do not paste official report or question text here.

## Paper shape

Five questions in three parts: Part 1 (questions 1–2), Part 2 (questions 3–4), and Part 3 (question 5).

## Recurring gaps

- Sitting too early, with bachelor-level descriptions of work instead of further learning applied to this port scheme, was a main theme.
- Generic textbook theory, pasted contract or sustainability-goal text, long answers that may have been AI-assisted, and weak syllabus knowledge did not compensate for missing the question.
- Write in the first person as the named role, and plan the time across all five questions.

## By question

- Q1: A frequent trap was to display syllabus knowledge and still miss the core task: leading a team to choose a cost-effective, buildable, and sustainable option for this client.
- Q2: The standpoint is a design-and-construct tender team assuring the client, not the usual client-side procurement essay. Few answers made that shift.
- Q3: This question was generally handled more strongly when constructability and design expertise were tied to the chosen breakwater solution.
- Q4: Better answers treated this as structured health-and-safety management, not a loose hazard list.
- Q5: Some answers stayed on immediate site practicalities and underplayed the second limb: how you would assure the client on cost, time, and quality after the storm.
